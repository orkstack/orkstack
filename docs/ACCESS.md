# Access: permissions, roles and bindings

With `auth.mode` set to `observe` or `enforce` ([AUTHENTICATION.md](./AUTHENTICATION.md)),
every request is decided against the deployment's **access document**: who
holds which **role** at which **scope**. This page is the reference for the
permissions, the built-in roles, the document and how each RPC is decided.
In `asserted` mode, the default, none of it runs.

The catalogue has **57** permissions, and **114** procedures have a row
that decides them. Both numbers are generated by the catalogue's own test
(`internal/api/testdata/access_numbers.golden`), so they move with the code.

## Permissions

A permission is `<resource>.<verb>`. The catalogue is closed and compiled
in: a custom role lists permissions by exact name, with no wildcard, so a
permission a later release adds is never granted by accident.
`orkctl` and the API list it (`AccessService.ListPermissions`).

A permission is held at a **scope kind**, and a binding grants a
permission only at a scope that covers it:

| Scope | Written | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| deployment | `deployment` | everything |
| namespace | `namespace:<ns>` | that namespace and every Orkflow in it, with their runs, schedules and triggers |
| Orkflow | `orkflow:<ns>/<name>` | that Orkflow, its runs, schedules and triggers |
| pool | `pool:<pool>` | that pool |

A role bound at a scope grants only its permissions of that scope's kind
or narrower: `namespace.create`, a deployment permission, is never granted
by a binding at a namespace, whatever role it names.

| Permission | Held at | Allows |
|---|---|---|
| `orkflow.validate` | namespace | validate an Orkflow document without storing it |
| `orkflow.write` | Orkflow | create an Orkflow or store a new version of it |
| `orkflow.read` | Orkflow | read an Orkflow, its versions, callers and plans |
| `orkflow.promote` | Orkflow | move an Orkflow's current version |

a secret is most likely to be found. All three ways to a task's output ask
for `run.logs.read`: the live stream, `ReadArtifact` of `STDOUT` or
`STDERR`, and an evidence bundle that does not exclude its logs.

## Built-in roles

Built-in roles are defined in code, never stored, and their names are
reserved. `orkctl` shows them with the document; their exact sets are held
to `internal/authz/testdata/catalogue.golden`.

| Role | Meant for | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `viewer` | read-only access | `orkflow.read`, `run.read`, `schedule.read`, `trigger.read`, `eventsource.read`, `webhook.read`, `stats.read`, `namespace.read`; bound at the deployment also `orker.read` and `system.read` |
| `operator` | running and unblocking | `viewer`, and `run.submit`, `run.cancel`, `run.await.resolve`, `run.logs.read`, `run.artifacts.read`, `run.resume`, `run.pause`, `schedule.operate`, `eventsource.operate`, `eventsource.logs.read`, `webhook.redeliver` |
| `developer` | authoring | `operator`, and `orkflow.validate`, `orkflow.write`, `orkflow.promote`, `schedule.write`, `trigger.write`, `eventsource.write`, `cache.purge`, `automation.plan`, `run.evidence.export` |
| `namespace-admin` | a tenant's own administration | `developer`, and `orkflow.delete`, `webhook.write`, `namespace.update`, `audit.read`, `access.bind`, `run.cancel.force`, `run.cancel.bulk`, `concurrency.reset`, `payload.keys.manage` |
| `auditor` | evidence | every `*.read` but `run.logs.read`, `run.artifacts.read` and `eventsource.logs.read`, and `run.evidence.export` |
| `admin` | the deployment | every permission but an Orker's four |
| `orker` | an Orker, at pool scope | `identity.token.mint`, `task.result.commit`, `payload.key.use`, `eventsource.host` |

`namespace.policy.update` and `run.history.decode` are `admin`'s alone: a
tenant's administrator runs the namespace without being able to widen what
it may reach.

## The access document

One object, `metadata/identity/access.json`, holds the custom roles, the
bindings, the service accounts and the disabled principals. Only an engine
writes it, by compare-and-swap on the etag a read returned.

```yaml
roles:
  - name: release-manager
    permissions:
      - orkflow.read
      - orkflow.promote
bindings:
  - name: payments-developers
    role: developer
    scope: namespace:payments
    subjects:
      - group:corp:payments-eng
  - name: deploy-from-ci
    role: operator
    scope: orkflow:payments/deploy
    subjects:
      - claims:github:repository=acme/payments,ref=refs/heads/main
disabled:
  - user:corp:mallory@acme.dev
```

(Shown as YAML; it is stored as JSON.)

### Subjects

A binding names its subjects from a closed set:

| Subject | Matches |
|---|---|
| `user:<issuer>:<name>` | that person |
| `group:<issuer>:<group>` | a person whose groups include it |
| `claims:<issuer>:<k>=<v>,…` | a service of that issuer whose kept claims all equal the values; `sub` is always selectable |
| `serviceaccount:<name>` | that service account |
| `cert:<name>` | that certificate principal |

A value is literal, or ends in one `*` that matches any remainder. A value of
only `*`, a `*` anywhere but the end, a `claims:` subject with no selector or
with only wildcards, and a selector on a claim the issuer does not keep
(its `claims` list) are refused.

A principal under `disabled` is refused whatever it presents
(`PRINCIPAL_DISABLED`), and an open stream of it is closed.

### Bounds and validation

256 custom roles, 4096 bindings, 64 subjects a binding, 256 service
accounts, 4 MiB in all. Names are slugs, validated and never sanitized. A
write is refused for an unknown permission, a binding to an unknown role, a
namespace scope naming no namespace (an Orkflow or pool scope that does not
exist yet is a warning), a stored field that is output only, and a change
that would leave nobody but `auth.admins` able to hold `access.write` while
`auth.admins` is empty.

### Bindings from configuration

`auth.admins` binds `admin` at the deployment, and `auth.orkers`
(`ORK_AUTH_ORKERS`) binds the `orker` role at a pool, from the file that
configures the engine, before anyone has signed in:

```yaml
auth:
  orkers:
    - subject: cert:spiffe://acme.dev/orker/gpu-*
      pool: gpu
    - subject: serviceaccount:orker-main
      pool: main
      orker_ids: any
```

They are in force whatever the document says, are listed as from
configuration, and no write removes them; the boot refuses a subject or a
pool that is not one. `orker_ids: any` is for a pool whose Orkers share one
credential: the engine then takes the Orker id the request's attempt names,
so one stolen Orker of that pool can claim to be any other. Every engine
runs the same `auth.orkers`.

### Changing it

Each `AccessService` write changes one role, binding, service account or
disabled principal: it reads the document, changes that one thing,
validates the whole, writes the revision and swaps the head. A request
carries the etag its caller read; if the document has moved since, nothing
is written and the request is `aborted`, so read it again and retry. A
request that carries no etag is retried from a fresh read three times, then
`aborted`. A write that changes nothing writes nothing and answers the same
etag. The replica that wrote decides from the new document at once; the
others within `auth.policy_refresh`.

Writes need `access.write` at the deployment, with one exception: a holder
of `access.bind` at a namespace creates and deletes bindings there, at the
namespace or one of its Orkflows, for a role whose every permission the
binding would grant it holds there itself. It cannot bind `admin`, the
deployment or a pool, a role whose binding would grant nothing at that
scope (`orker`, at a namespace), or a role with a permission it lacks; a
deployment permission a role holds is inert at a namespace and is not asked
of it. Only `access.write` updates a binding. The decision is made against
the exact document the write replaces. A delegated binding may name any
subject, a group or a claims pattern of any trusted issuer included, and it
grants whatever its role grants later: a custom role widened by
`UpdateRole` widens every binding of it, delegated or not.

`SetPrincipalDisabled` does not disable break-glass, a principal
`auth.admins` makes admin, or the caller itself: a disabled principal holds
nothing, configuration's grants included, and either would leave access to
be repaired in the bucket. An Orker `auth.orkers` binds is disabled like any
other principal.

A service account's keys are named by their RFC 7638 thumbprint, and each
records when it was added.

### In the web UI

Admin → Access shows the document and changes it through the same
`AccessService` calls: **Bindings** (with the banners of the document's
state and every binding's pin, configuration's shown read only), **Roles**
(built-in read only; custom ones with the catalogue's permissions grouped by
resource), **Service accounts** (a key made in the browser has its private
half offered as a file named `<account>-<first 12 characters of its kid>.pem`,
again on asking, and its public half is stored only once you say the file is
saved; the private half is kept nowhere), **People** (who is signed in now,
disabled, or named by a binding; disable, enable, end sign-ins; disabling or
signing out yourself says it is you), **No access** (a person signed in and
bound to nothing sees their principal, issuer and groups, and copies them for
an administrator),
**Explain** and **History** (any two revisions diffed; a revision written
around the API acknowledged). A control the caller lacks the permission for
is disabled, with the permission it needs beside it; the server still
decides every request. Changing or deleting a binding you hold a role by says
so first, and a write refused because the document changed since the page
read it offers to read it again, keeping what you entered. In `asserted` mode the page says authentication is
off.

### Pinned bindings

A namespace or an Orkflow deleted and created again under its name is a new
thing with an old name. Each has an immutable id (`ns_…`, `of_…`; `default`
is `ns_default`), and a binding is pinned to the one its scope named when it
was saved, so it does not carry over to a namespace or an Orkflow that only
shares the name.

| A binding whose scope | Is stored | Matches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| names a namespace or an Orkflow that exists when it is saved | with that thing's id (`scope_id`) | that incarnation only. After a delete and a create under the name it matches nothing, and is listed as stale |
| names one that does not exist yet (a pipeline granted `orkflow.write` before its first create) | with no id | by name, listed as "by name". Its next save, once the thing exists, pins it |
| is the deployment or a pool | with no id | as before: neither is made again under a name |

- Pinning again is a named act: `orkctl admin binding update NAME --repin`
  (`UpdateBinding` with `repin`), by someone who may update the binding. It
  is refused when nothing exists at the scope, and for a binding at the
  deployment or a pool. A save that changes nothing else never moves a pin;
  a save that changes the scope pins to what the new scope names.
- `orkctl admin binding list` shows each binding's pin: `pinned ns_…`, `by
  name`, or `stale` with the command that pins it again. `ExplainAccess`
  names a binding that would have matched by name and did not because it is
  pinned to an earlier incarnation.
- "A namespace with bindings cannot be deleted" still stands. The pin is
  what covers a delete that went round it: an engine of an earlier release,
  or a writer of the bucket.
- An id is opaque, and no API takes one where it takes a name.
- A binding that matches by name grants its subjects whatever is made under
  that name: a binding saved before a namespace or an Orkflow exists, and a
  binding saved before this release, until its next save. Whoever may create
  that namespace or Orkflow chooses what the binding then covers. Save such a
  binding again once its scope exists, or bind only what exists.
- A request about a run is judged against the id of the namespace and the
  Orkflow as they are now, not as they were when the run started. A run of
  an Orkflow that was deleted and made again is read under the bindings of
  the new one.

A decision that a pinned binding could take part in (one in a namespace
where any binding is pinned) reads the ids of what the request names. One
replica reuses an id it read for 10 seconds, the policy cache's own window,
so a namespace made again through another replica may be decided against
its old id for that long ([AUTHENTICATION.md](AUTHENTICATION.md), "How old a
decision's facts may be"). While the ids cannot be read the request is
`unavailable`, and so is a list filtered by row: a pinned binding is never
decided as if no id existed, and a list never answers with rows left out
that it could not decide. What the caller is told names what failed, never
the store; the engine's log holds the detail.

Every write is audited (`access.role.create`, `.update`, `.delete`;
`access.binding.*`; `access.service-account.*`; `access.principal.disable`,
`.enable`), its detail naming the revision it produced.

From the command line: `orkctl admin role list|get|create|update|delete`,
`orkctl admin binding list|create|update|delete` (`--role`, `--scope`,
`--subject`), `orkctl admin service-account list|get|create|delete` and
`key add|remove` (`create --generate-key FILE` writes the private key once,
mode 0600), and `orkctl admin principal disable|enable`. Each reads the
document first and sends back its etag.

### Explaining a decision, and the history

`ExplainAccess` decides one permission for one principal against the
replica's copy of the document, names the binding that granted it, and
lists every binding that names the principal or covers the resource with why
it did not: the subject differs, the scope differs, the role lacks the
permission at that scope, or the binding is pinned elsewhere. A denial's
reason is `NO_BINDING` or `PRINCIPAL_DISABLED`. A principal is known there by
its id alone, so a binding that selects by group or by a claim other than
`sub` is shown as not matching it.

`ListAccessRevisions` lists the history newest first: the head as it is,
then each revision found by following the links back from it;
`GetAccessRevision` reads one by number, the head included, at most 4096
revisions behind the head. A head written around the API that has no
revision of its own is still listed, with its digest. `orkctl admin access
explain PRINCIPAL PERMISSION [--namespace N] [--orkflow O] [--pool P]`,
`orkctl admin access history` (revision, digest, when, who, change) and
`orkctl admin access diff FROM TO` are their commands.

### Revisions, and a document written around the API

Before the head moves, the new document is written create-only to
`metadata/identity/access-revisions/{revision:012}-{sha256}.json`, and each
revision names the SHA-256 of the one before it. A revision is a record:
nothing reads one to decide anything. A lost compare-and-swap leaves an
orphan revision that history, which follows the links back from the head,
never shows.

A head that has no revision, or whose chain does not lead back to the
revision a replica last saw, was written around the API (a bucket writer, a
restore from backup). The replica uses it, logs at error, and reports it
(`AccessDocument.written_around_api`) until an admin acknowledges it.

`AcknowledgeAccessRevision` (`access.write`; `orkctl admin access
acknowledge REVISION SHA256`) acknowledges the revision a replica found
written around the API, or the head as it is now, named by its number and
digest as the history lists them; any other is refused
`failed_precondition`. It writes one create-only record,
`metadata/identity/access-acks/{revision:012}-{sha256}.json`, naming who
acknowledged it and when; a second acknowledgement of the same revision
keeps the first record and succeeds. A replica that finds a head written
around the API looks for that head's record, or one for the head it has
now, first, and again at every read while the banner is up, so an
acknowledgement made through one replica clears the banner on all of them
within `auth.policy_refresh`. A store that cannot be read clears nothing. A
document removed around the API names no revision to acknowledge: its
banner clears once a document is written again and that head is
acknowledged.
`AccessStatus.acknowledged_revision` is the highest revision a record
names. A record decides whether the banner shows, never access: no decision
reads one, and a bucket writer who can write one can as well write the head
it hides.

### Every replica's copy

Each replica holds the compiled document in memory and revalidates it with
one conditional read every `auth.policy_refresh` (10 s). A change reaches
every replica within that bound, and every decision names the revision it
was made against. When the store cannot be read the copy is served stale for
at most `auth.policy_stale_limit` (5 min; `0` closes at once). Past it every
request is refused `unavailable`, except those of `auth.admins` and
break-glass, which live in configuration. A binding removed while the store
is unreachable stays effective on a replica for that window.

## How a request is decided

One interceptor, first in the chain, decides every RPC, unary and
streaming, from its procedure's row: the permissions it asks, and where the
resource comes from in the request. A procedure with no row is refused.
The run socket and the task log socket are decided by `StreamEvents`' and
`StreamTaskLogs`' rows; the legacy `/v1/webhooks` routes need the webhook
permissions at the deployment.

| Service | Decided |
|---|---|
| Orkflow | `orkflow.*` at the Orkflow the request names; `ApplyOrkflow` at the document's name; `ValidateOrkflow` at the namespace |
| Run | `run.read` (a run's reads), `run.logs.read`, `run.cancel`, `run.await.resolve` at the run's Orkflow; `ReadArtifact` by kind (`STDOUT`/`STDERR` `run.logs.read`, `OUTPUT` `run.artifacts.read`, `VALUES` `run.read`); `GetArtifactProvenance` `run.read` and the same by kind;
`ExportRunEvidence` `run.evidence.export`, and `run.logs.read` unless `exclude_logs` and `run.artifacts.read` unless `exclude_artifacts`; `DiffRuns` `run.read` on both; `SubmitRun` `run.submit`, and `run.read` on a `source_run_id` |
| Schedule, Trigger | the object's permission at its Orkflow; a create or update also `run.submit`; a fire or backfill `schedule.operate` and `run.submit` |
| Webhook | at the subscription's scope (its namespace, or the deployment when unscoped); a register or update `webhook.write` and `run.read`, at the old scope and the new |
| Stats, Cache, Automation, Namespace | at the namespace; `PurgeCache` with `all` at the deployment; `UpdateNamespace` also `namespace.policy.update` when a policy field changes, decided by the handler against the exact document its compare-and-swap replaces; `CreateNamespace` and `DeleteNamespace` at the deployment |
| Activity, System, Audit, Access | at the deployment; `ListAuditRecords` also of one namespace with a namespace filter |

**A request that carries a document is decided where the document acts.**
`ApplyOrkflow`, `ValidateOrkflow`, `PlanRun` with a document and
`PlanAutomation` are decided in the namespace the document names, else the
request's, else `default`. Only the document's top-level `namespace:` and
`name:` are read, as plain strings; a document whose head cannot be read that
way is decided at the deployment. A request that names another namespace than
its document is refused `invalid_argument` before anything is read
(`PlanAutomation` answers it with its namespace issue). A composed task's
missing version lists the versions only of an Orkflow the caller may read.

**A run's scope is its Orkflow.** A caller who may read the namespace is
decided there and nothing more is read; otherwise the run's Orkflow is read
from the same facts a run read returns. A run the caller may not read is
answered `not_found`, exactly as one that does not exist. A shared child
run's Orkflow is `<owner>/<name>`, which no Orkflow binding of the
namespace names.

**Lists.** A caller who holds the permission on the whole scope the request
names gets the list unchanged. Otherwise `ListSchedules`, `ListTriggers` and
`ListWebhooks` leave out the rows the caller may not read. A list paged by a
key walk or by a cursor that names its last row needs a filter, equals or
in, whose every value the caller may read, or it is refused
`permission_denied` naming the filter to add: `ListRuns` an `orkflow_name`
filter, `ListOrkflows` and `ListNamespaces` a `name` filter, and
`ListAuditRecords` and `ListDeliveries` a `namespace` filter. So no page and
no page token names a row the caller may not see. `ListDeliveries` also
passes over the deliveries of a webhook the caller may not read, so a page
fills, and its token ends, on rows it may. `ListRunArtifacts` needs
`run.read` and leaves out the logs and artifacts the caller may not read.
`ListOrkflowCallers` names only the composing Orkflows the caller may read,
and the triggers and schedules only with `trigger.read` and `schedule.read`
at the Orkflow; `scanned_versions` counts those definitions alone.

**Open streams.** A stream or socket is decided again at every change of the
access document. One whose principal was disabled, or lost the permission,
is closed with a WebSocket close of status 1008, or on a Connect stream
`permission_denied` when it lost the permission, `unauthenticated` when its
principal was disabled or the credential it was opened with expired, and
`unavailable` when the document is past its stale limit. A task's log can stream for hours; a decision made at the first
byte does not outlive its revocation.

Past the stale limit, configuration's principals still authorize, and a
principal the last copy of the document disabled stays disabled.

## Denials and the audit trail

In `enforce` mode a denial answers `permission_denied` (`unauthenticated`
for a caller nobody authenticated) naming the principal, the permission and
the scope, never a binding. In `observe` mode nothing is refused but
`AccessService`, which is enforced in every mode: the decision is computed
and recorded as `would-deny`. A caller refused at a namespace learns nothing
from a lookup: a missing schedule, trigger or delivery is answered with the
same refusal as one that exists.

Every denial is recorded, of a read as well (`access.denied`), with the
permission, the scope and the decision. At most one record per principal,
procedure and scope a minute on a replica, and at most ten per principal a
minute, are written; the limiter is in memory and a restart forgets it, so
the count of records is a floor. A caller nobody authenticated writes no
record.

Outside `asserted` mode every audit record is schema version 2: beside the
principal it names whether it was verified, the method, the issuer, the
permission, the scope, the decision, the binding that admitted it (`rule`,
`binding:<name>` or `config:admins`) and the document's revision. In
`asserted` mode records stay version 1, byte for byte.
